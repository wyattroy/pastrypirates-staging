## 5. Geometry lessons paid for (don't re-learn)

- `offsetPoly` is a mitre offset; near-parallel tiny segments shoot the join to infinity (mitre
  limit added); offsetting INWARD past a sliver folds the polygon — a notch plus a band around it
  must fit in the straight run between two rounded corners.
- `flatten()` must honour a circle's direction or a flattened ring fills solid when clipped.
- Raster shapes never overlap; holes are reversed sub-paths under nonzero (LightBurn fill modes
  cancel overlaps). Wedge polygons must be traced clockwise or their cut-outs vanish.
- Kerf: outward on outlines, inward on holes, decided by containment within the same `piece`.
- `notchPolyline` rotates the polyline to start far from the notch (a window straddling index 0
  otherwise deletes everything).
- Tracer: per-asset ink thresholds; "not red" keeps the cocoa wrapper open; morphological close
  heals the egg's nick; padded variants open the silhouette first or sparkle pixels grow blobs;
  `inkInset` drops ink near a coast; `blackbg` for the Chrome-rendered emoji.
- Chest hinge: both halves' holes 3.3 mm from their edge, tongue ends r 3.1 about the axle, strip
  body 2.4 → lid wall 9 mm = 3.3 + 2.4 + 3.3. Side play 0.15 per tongue.

## 8c. His caliper numbers, and the one-offs (2026-08-25, later)

From the uncompensated test pieces: **dock tab 8.7** (kerf 0.30 on outer cuts), **island notch 9.4**
(kerf 0.25 on holes), **"6 mm" ply is really 5.9**. KERF's default is now **0.275** — the average,
because with one compensation value the tab and socket errors cancel and every joint lands on its
designed play. MAT = 5.9. **MAT3 = 2.6 — his caliper number for the "3 mm" ply (same day), a 0.4 mm lie by the label.**
Kerf 0.275 was measured on the 5.9 mm stock; the first thin-ply cut is the coupon for thin kerf —
the sail tab is drawn 6.9 wide; if it calipers under 6.9, split KERF by material rather than
re-averaging.

## 8b. THE FIRST WOOD (2026-08-25) — and the kerf lesson

He cut islands, docks and tokens — the set exists physically. The docks "totally wiggle" (~0.5 mm):
he had cut the GROUP files, which carried NO kerf compensation (that convention said "cut from the
sheets" in an SVG <desc> nobody sees in Rhino). Since then every cut file is compensated and the
dock-tab side play went 0.15 → 0.05 so it snaps. Terminology settled with him: KERF is what the beam
eats (a measured machine property); what we set are the COMPENSATION (assumed 0.18 until measured)
and the designed PLAY. Same ruling for the 3 mm pieces ("the treasure chests, crates, and ships should also snap"): the
ships' hull slots and the spinner ring's vane slot went MAT3 + 0.2 → MAT3 + 0.05, the sail tabs'
lengthwise play 0.3 → 0.1. The box joints of crates and chests were already drawn line-on-line —
they only wiggled because the group files carried no compensation, so the kerf fix alone makes them
press-fit; nothing else to change there. The chest HINGE keeps its 0.15/tongue — it has to rotate.
NOTE: MAT3 = 3 exactly; "3 mm" ply is often 2.7–3.0 — his caliper number for the ply thickness
matters as much as the kerf (--material for 6 mm, and MAT3 in the source for 3 mm).

He is measuring the cut pieces with calipers: from the uncompensated test,
real kerf = 9.00 − (tab width) = (notch width) − 9.15. When his numbers arrive, regenerate with
`--kerf <measured>` — that is the whole change.

## 8a. DXF: raster arrives FILLED — and ezdxf writes the files (2026-08-25)

Wyatt: "your dxf files don't correctly save the black raster area as filled in." First fix: a
hand-written R2000 with HATCH entities, "validated" with ezdxf in RECOVER mode — **a check that
cannot fail the way Rhino fails, and it didn't: Rhino refused the files outright** ("Opendesign
error: null object id ... Can't recover file"). ODA wants the full R2000 object structure.

So the generator no longer writes DXF bytes at all: `generate.mjs` hands the geometry as JSON to
`art/dxf.py`, and **ezdxf writes the files** (R2000; RASTER = one solid HATCH per item, loops =
its sub-paths, odd-parity so holes stay open; CUT = bare polylines/circles). Validation is now
strict-mode `ezdxf.readfile` + audit, zero errors — and the bytes come FROM the reference library
rather than being blessed by it. **One-time setup on a new machine: `pip3 install --user ezdxf`**
(generate.mjs fails loudly without it). Stale crates-a/b and markers files were deleted from
v3-round when the A/B options went. He has not yet re-tried Rhino on these.
